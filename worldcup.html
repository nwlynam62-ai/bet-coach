USA 2–0 Australia (Grp D) — the hosts clinched Group D with a game to spare, a second straight win after the 4–1 opener. Switzerland 4–1 Bosnia-Herzegovina (Grp B) — the Swiss answered with a rout of their own to stay level with Canada on four points.

Yesterday · Wed Jun 17 — Groups K & L open

Portugal 1–1 DR Congo (Grp K) — João Neves headed Portugal ahead inside five minutes, but Yoane Wissa leveled on the stroke of half-time; Cristiano Ronaldo started, missed two big chances and stretched his major-tournament goalless run to 10 games. Colombia 3–1 Uzbekistan (Grp K) — Daniel Muñoz, Luis Díaz and a 90+9′ Jhon Campaz strike saw off the debutants, whose Abbosbek Fayzullaev grabbed Uzbekistan’s first-ever World Cup goal. England 4–2 Croatia (Grp L) — Harry Kane bagged a brace (12′ pen, 42′) with Jude Bellingham and Marcus Rashford adding to a chaotic win; Martin Baturina and Petar Musa twice pegged England back. Ghana 1–0 Panama (Grp L) — Caleb Yirenkyi’s stoppage-time header (90+5′) snatched a priceless opener for Carlos Queiroz’s side.

Yesterday · Tue Jun 16 — the headliners deliver

Groups I & J opened and the superstars all struck. France 3–1 Senegal (Grp I) — Kylian Mbappé scored twice, including a 29-metre rocket, becoming France’s all-time leading scorer (58) and moving to 14 World Cup goals, third-most ever. Norway 4–1 Iraq (Grp I) — Erling Haaland marked his World Cup debut with a first-half brace; Norway lead Group I on goal difference. Argentina 3–0 Algeria (Grp J) — Lionel Messi, in a record sixth World Cup, scored his first-ever World Cup hat trick. Austria 1–0 Jordan (Grp J) — a 20th-minute goal settled a tight one.

How we're covering it

Every match, every day, on the board. From June 11 the main board gets a World Cup tab — each match ranked by confidence with a full three-way moneyline breakdown and a total breakdown.

The draw is the blind spot. A third of group-stage matches historically end level, and casual money never prices it. Our match breakdowns treat the draw as a first-class outcome — including when draw-no-bet or double-chance is the smarter shape.

Honesty about the market: World Cup main lines are the sharpest in all of sports. Our reads run paper-only through the group stage while we measure them against the toughest competition there is. Where soft spots actually live: rotation news on clinched teams, heat and altitude (the Azteca sits at 7,200 feet), dead-rubber third games, and prop markets.

Late team news

Final 26-man squads locked Jun 1, announced Jun 2; a player can still be swapped for injury up to 24h before a team’s first match. Sources: ESPN injuries tracker, Sky Sports & Bundesliga, 8–9 Jun 2026. Squad gaps are exactly the kind of soft spot the razor-sharp main lines can be slow to price.

Germany lose a teenager. Bayern’s 18-year-old Lennart Karl — one of the brightest young names in any squad — tore a thigh muscle in training and is out, replaced by RB Leipzig’s Assan Ouédraogo. A depth hit rather than a spine hit for a side already favored to cruise Group E.

Netherlands lose a starter. Jurriën Timber withdrew Jun 8 with a lingering groin issue, replaced by Lutsharel Geertruida — a real downgrade at the back for a Dutch side in a tight Group F.

Spain: one out, one back. Fermín López is out after fracturing a metatarsal, but Mikel Merino has recovered from the foot problem that had sidelined him since January — close to a wash for the tournament co-favorite.
