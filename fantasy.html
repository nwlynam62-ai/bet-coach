Fantasy advice from the sharpest source there is: the betting market.

Rankings sites guess. The market puts money on it. This section reads Vegas win totals, team totals, and player prop markets to find the fantasy values the consensus is sleeping on — and the landmines it's still drafting too high.

How this works

1. Win totals set the table. Offenses attached to high win totals score more, lead more, and feed more fantasy points. We start every team outlook from the number, not the narrative.

2. Props are projections with money behind them. When sportsbooks post a player's yardage and TD props, that's a professional projection you can compare straight against his draft cost.

3. ADP vs the market is the edge. When drafters price a player far below what the prop market implies — that's a pick. Far above — that's a fade. We'll publish both lists all draft season.

The WFL lens — 13-team · full PPR · 6-pt pass TD

Format corrections every public board gets wrong for your league

1. Every market ADP here is half-PPR (Underdog). Your league pays full point per catch: bump high-catch players up — Reed, McConkey, Wan'Dale-profile slots, pass-catching RBs, 100-catch TEs (Bowers, McBride) are all worth MORE than the boards above show.

2. 6-pt passing TDs: QBs are worth more than any public board says. The spread between elite QBs widens (a 40-TD season gains +60–80 pts on the field vs 4-pt scoring), and pocket volume passers gain the most relative value — Burrow and Mahomes are the format's biggest winners. The rushing-QB edge shrinks (a rush TD beats a pass TD by 1.5 pts here, not 3.5) — Lamar stays elite, but the gap behind him closes.

3. 13 teams: scarcity bites. ADP 100–150 players are starters in your league. TE13 is already replacement level — top-3 TE at value, or punt and churn; there is no middle. Roughly 2 QBs rostered per team means QB2s vanish — which is exactly why room-faller Mahomes at 133 is the format steal.

4. Six bench spots, no IR: no handcuffs. The only peer-reviewed test (1,350 leagues) found handcuffing gives ZERO win-rate benefit (51.0% vs 50.6%). Bench = upside swings on 10.5+ win-total offenses. K/DST in the last two rounds, always — CBS autopilot teams take theirs in rounds 9–11 and donate skill depth to you.

Roster guardrails — the anti-dogma checkpoints

What's worked in all five years: the shape, not the strategy

Zero-RB won Best Ball Mania in 2023; RB-heavy won it in 2024. No fixed draft dogma survives contact with a new season. What has beaten average scoring in all five years isn't a strategy — it's a positional shape at each checkpoint. Hit these counts and you're never the galaxy-brained roster that blows up; miss one and that's your signal to correct — not a reason to panic-reach past ADP.

By Rd 6
3 WR2 RB+1 best value
The one constant across every BBM season. Full PPR (your league) makes the 3-WR lean stronger, not weaker. The 6th pick is open — take the best market value (often a falling TE1, QB, or 3rd RB).
By Rd 10
4–5 WR4 RBTE1 in
Diminishing returns kick in — you can only start 3 WR + flex. 4 RB through Rd 10 has been top-2 in 3 straight years. Have your TE1 by here unless you punted to churn (per the WFL lens).
Final 14
6 WR4–5 RB1–2 TE1–2 QBKDST
Your redraft roster compresses best ball's 6–7 WR / 5–6 RB / 2–3 TE into 12 skill picks + K/DST in the last two rounds.

Why these differ from the best-ball numbers: the 5-year study is 18-round best ball with no waivers and no K/DST. Your WFL is 14 roster spots, weekly waivers, and a required kicker + defense. So compress the back end: skip the 7th WR / 6th RB hoarding (waivers cover depth — bench is for upside swings on 10.5+ offenses), and pull your QB1 deadline forward to ~Rd 12 — a 13-team pool thins the startable-QB tier faster than best ball's Rd-14 cliff.

Why tiers, not rankings

The best draft tools (FantasyPros' Draft Wizard is the industry standard) all converge on the same insight: drafters don't need rank #23 vs #24 — they need to know where the cliffs are. Tiers tell you when to reach and when to wait. Ours differ in one way: the tier breaks come from betting-market numbers with real money behind them, not staff consensus. The same tiering ESPN’s and Yahoo’s draft kits build by hand — Yahoo’s own cheat sheets are “tiered by position to show where value drops” — now runs live on the positional board below, every cliff drawn straight from market ADP.

Market stacks — correlation the books point to

Stack the offenses the market believes in — where the pieces are still cheap

A QB and his pass-catchers rise and fall together, and across a season the whole offense correlates if the team is efficient and winning — which is exactly what a high win total flags. So the market already tells you which offenses are worth stacking. The edge is finding the ones whose secondary pieces are still discounted vs ADP.
